A three-round Who's the Spy tournament: points, tiebreaks and a winner

Ahmed Moaz · 16 November 2026

A single game of Who's the Spy is a few minutes long. Three rounds make a small tournament, with a winner. This is how the points and the format work.

The default

The game runs three rounds with one spy. The highest score after the last round wins.

The points

  1. Voting for a spy scores 1.
  2. A spy whom nobody votes for scores 1.
  3. A lone spy who names the word gets 1 more.

A worked example

Take a table of five. In round one the spy is caught by three of the four others, who score 1 each. The spy, caught and alone, guesses the word right and scores 1. In round two a different player is the spy, and nobody votes for them: the spy scores 1, and no civilian scores. In round three the spy is caught by two civilians, who score 1 each, and the spy misses the guess.

That gives the points across three rounds: civilians who voted correctly have 1 or 2, the spies have 0 to 2. It is close, which is the aim.

The fair spy draw

Nobody is the spy twice running unless the table is too small, and a player who has been spy often is less likely to be chosen again.

Running it

  1. Choose three rounds. It is the default.
  2. Pick packs from 25, or add your own words.
  3. Read the scores aloud after each round.
  4. Declare a winner at the end, with applause.

If there is a tie

Play one more round, with a fresh word, and see who is ahead. It is quicker than arguing.

Length

Boardit's table log, from 13 September to 30 October 2026, shows 7.1 minutes for the median finished game. Three rounds fit into a short evening.

Where to play

Run the tournament on Boardit, online with three to eight players.

With two spies

A tournament can use two spies. With two spies at a table of eight each player has a one in four chance of being a spy, against one in eight with one, so the balance of a round changes before anyone speaks.

The format in a line

Three rounds, one spy and a point for every good call.
