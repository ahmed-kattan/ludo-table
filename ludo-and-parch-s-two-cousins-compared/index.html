Ludo and Parchís: two cousins compared

Ahmed Moaz · 14 November 2026

Ludo and Parchís look alike, and they come from the same ancestor. They also differ in ways that change how the game feels. These are the facts, from Wikipedia's articles on both, which I read on 17 October 2026.

Parchís

Parchís is Spanish, adapted from Pachisi. The standard board has 68 spaces and four colours. Up to four people play, with four pawns each. A five releases a pawn. A six gives an extra turn. With four pawns out, a six moves seven. A capturing pawn advances 20, and a pawn reaching the end lets another advance 10.

Ludo

Ludo was marketed in England in 1896 by Alfred Collier under the name Royal Ludo. The track on Boardit has 52 squares, with eight safe squares, and a piece travels 56 steps from its entry square to home. A six brings a piece out. A six, a capture or a piece home earns another throw. Three sixes in a row lose the turn.

What the differences do

In Parchís a capture or an arrival moves a piece a fixed number of steps, which is a bonus you can plan. In Ludo it gives another throw, which is a bonus you cannot plan. Parchís has more squares, so it is a longer game.

The common ancestor

Both grow from Pachisi, a cross-shaped game from India played with cowrie shells. Differences came as the game travelled.

Which to play

If you like a bonus you can plan, try Parchís. If you like a quick game with chance, Ludo.

Why the differences exist

Games change as they travel. A rule that suits one country, such as a bonus move after a capture, becomes part of the local game, and a neighbour tweaks it again. Neither version is wrong. They are the same idea tuned by different players over many years, and that is true of almost every board game that has lasted.
