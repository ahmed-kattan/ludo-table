How many rent payments does a Property Rush city need to pay for itself?

Ahmed Moaz · 7 November 2026

A city is an investment, and you can count how long it takes to pay back. I did the arithmetic with the stop rates from my simulation of the board, which I ran on 17 October 2026.

The method

For each city, I took its price and its rent, and multiplied the rent by the chance that an opponent's token stops there. That gives the income per opponent stop. Then I divided the price by it. The answer is how many opponent stops it takes for rent alone to pay back the price.

The results for bare land

  • Toronto, price 400, base rent 50: about 293 opponent stops.
  • Osaka: about 368.
  • Paris: about 446.
  • The cheapest city, which has a rent of only two, takes over 1,400.

Those numbers are big. With only bare land, a city takes a very long time to pay for itself.

What doubling and building do

Owning a whole country doubles its rent before any building, so each of those figures is halved. Then buildings change everything. With three houses on every city of a country, and counting the price of the houses, Toronto pays back in about 26 stops and Osaka in about 28.

That is the whole game in two numbers. A bare city is a slow investment. A built-up country is a fast one.

What I would do

Buy complete sets where you can, and build early. Money in your pocket pays nothing. Money in houses on a country your opponents keep landing on pays back within a few dozen stops.

What to do with it

Count a city's payback before you decide to hold out for it. If the number is in the hundreds, the city is a stepping stone to a set, not a reason to keep cash. If it is in the dozens, as with a built-up country, spend what you have.

The cheap end of the board

The cheapest city, Abu Dhabi, costs 60 and pays a base rent of 2. In my table it takes about 1,400 opponent stops to pay for itself, which is longer than most games last. That is not a reason to skip it. Both UAE cities together cost 130, so a whole country is a cheap way to get your first doubled rent. It is the road to a first set, not an income.

The three quickest paybacks

The cheap end of the board is easy to read from the price list. Abu Dhabi costs 60 and its hotel rent is 250. Dubai costs 70 and its hotel rent is 450, 6.43 times its price, the highest ratio on the board. Toronto costs 400 and its base rent is 12.5% of that, the highest of any city; Abu Dhabi's base rent is 3.3%.

What the table hides

These are averages from a simulation of a single token. On a real board, tokens stop where players want to go, and cards change the spread. The numbers show the shape, not the schedule.
