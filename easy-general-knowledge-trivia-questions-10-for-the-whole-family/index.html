Easy general knowledge trivia questions: 10 for the whole family

Ahmed Moaz · 20 October 2026

A good family quiz starts with questions that everyone can try. These ten easy general knowledge trivia questions suit children, parents and grandparents, each answered with a note. Use them at the table after dinner, or as the opening round of a trivia quiz online with friends and relatives.

The questions

  1. How many sides does a hexagon have?
  2. How many legs does a spider have?
  3. Which planet is known as the Red Planet?
  4. Which country gave the Statue of Liberty to the United States?
  5. What shape is a stop sign in most countries?
  6. What is the tallest animal in the world?
  7. What is the largest mammal on Earth?
  8. Which famous puzzle has six coloured faces you twist to solve?
  9. Which sea creature has eight arms?
  10. What gas do humans need to breathe to stay alive?

The answers, with a note on each

  1. Six. Bees build honeycomb in hexagons because the shape holds the most honey for the least wax.
  2. Eight. Spiders are not insects; insects have six legs, and spiders are arachnids.
  3. Mars. It is red because its soil is full of iron oxide, the same stuff as rust.
  4. France. Gustave Eiffel, of Eiffel Tower fame, designed the statue's inner iron frame.
  5. An octagon. Its eight sides let drivers recognise it from behind, even covered in snow.
  6. The giraffe. Its neck has seven bones, the same number as a human neck.
  7. The blue whale. Its heart is roughly the size of a small car.
  8. The Rubik's Cube. Its Hungarian inventor, Ernő Rubik, took about a month to solve it.
  9. The octopus, which can change colour in a fraction of a second to hide from predators.
  10. Oxygen. Air is only about 21% oxygen; most of it is nitrogen.

How hard is the set?

Everything here is meant to be answered by an eight-year-old, and the notes are for the adults. If the table is too quick, ask for a second fact about each answer; the notes are a good source. The set is deliberately gentle, so use it to warm up before harder rounds.

How do you use it on the night?

Run it as a family trivia night opener: a ten-minute round before the main course of harder questions, while the plates are still on the table and the children are still fresh.

Why start with easy questions?

Because confidence is what makes a quiz fun. A table that gets its first five right will try the sixth with enthusiasm, and a table that fails the first question will want to leave. Easy questions are the host's way of saying that everyone is welcome. They also leave room for the notes, which are often surprising enough to win a gasp from the grown-ups.

How do you include the youngest?

Let them go first. Read a question, count to five and let the youngest player answer before the adults. If they are wrong, say 'good try' and give the real answer with the note. A child who feels clever will join in; a child who feels silly will not. If there are several children, give each a turn as quizmaster.

What can you do after the round?

Ask each person to make up one easy question for the next quiz. Children are brilliant at it, and their questions are often the funniest of the night. Collect them on a card, keep the card in the quiz box and use them next time. A family quiz that grows from the family's own questions will outlast any list.
