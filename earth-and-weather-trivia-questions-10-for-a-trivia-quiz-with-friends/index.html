Earth and weather trivia questions: 10 for a trivia quiz with friends

Ahmed Moaz · 24 October 2026

The planet under our feet is a great source of trivia questions: it has storms, volcanoes, giant waves and a surprising number of records. Here are ten Earth and weather trivia questions, from easy to hard. Use them at a table, or as a round in a trivia quiz online with friends.

The questions

  1. What do we call a huge ocean wave caused by an earthquake?
  2. What fraction of an iceberg is hidden under the water?
  3. A bolt of lightning is about how many times hotter than the surface of the Sun?
  4. What are the giant moving slabs that make up Earth's surface called?
  5. What was the supercontinent that existed about 300 million years ago?
  6. Which famous fault runs through California?
  7. What is a hot spring that shoots water into the air called?
  8. What causes the tides in the sea?
  9. Which place holds the record for the hottest temperature ever recorded on Earth, 56.7 degrees Celsius?
  10. Roughly what share of Earth's water is fresh water?

The answers, with a note on each

  1. A tsunami. The word is Japanese for harbour wave.
  2. About 90%. The rest is the tip that you can see.
  3. About five times hotter. A bolt is brief, but its heat is extreme.
  4. Tectonic plates. They move about as fast as fingernails grow.
  5. Pangaea, which means all lands in Greek.
  6. The San Andreas Fault, about 1,200 kilometres long.
  7. A geyser. The word comes from Geysir, a hot spring in Iceland.
  8. The Moon's gravity. Most coasts get two high tides a day.
  9. Death Valley. The record was set in 1913.
  10. About 3%. Most of Earth's fresh water is frozen in ice caps and glaciers.

How hard is the set?

The first three are friendly. Questions 4 to 8 are the middle ground, where a table of adults will argue. The last two are the sort of question where a number guess earns a laugh. If you have young players, swap in the questions about glaciers and tornadoes from the easy end of the topic.

How do you make it a competition?

Give each team a sheet and read the questions one at a time. Let them write an answer in thirty seconds. For the percentage questions, prize a point for any answer within ten per cent. That keeps the table close and the arguments friendly.

How do you fit it into a trivia night?

Use it as the science round of a longer trivia night, between a general knowledge round and a music round. A trivia game with six rounds of ten questions takes about an hour, and a set like this one fills a round neatly. Keep the trivia questions and answers on a sheet for the host, and read the notes after each answer; they are what people remember.

What makes a good Earth question?

A big number, a place you can picture and an answer that feels both surprising and obvious once you know it. The water question is a perfect example: most people guess far too high.

Ideas for a themed night

Pair this set with the one on space for a night of big things. Or follow it with a map quiz. People who like weather often like geography, and the transition feels natural.
