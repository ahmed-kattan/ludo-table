52 squares, 56 steps and a home column: how long a Ludo track really is

Ahmed Moaz · 12 November 2026

Ludo has a track, but the numbers on it are easy to mix up. These are the three that matter, with the reasons they differ.

52 squares on the track

The shared track has 52 squares. It is the ring that every piece goes around. Eight squares are safe: the four start squares where pieces enter, plus four stars. That is 15.4% of the track.

56 steps to home

A piece does not go all the way around the ring. It enters at its own start square and travels 56 steps to reach home. That takes it round most of the ring and then up its own column.

5 squares in the home column

The last stretch is a column of five squares that only your pieces use. Inside the home column a piece is out of reach, as rival pieces never enter it. This is why the last steps are a safe place and also the place where the exact number matters most. The final step to home needs the exact throw, so a piece in the column can wait.

What this means for a throw

Travelling start to home with a move on every throw takes 20.76 throws on average. The median is 19. Within 20 throws, 59.2% of pieces are home. Within 25, 83.6%. Within 30, 93.4%.

Those are numbers for a lone piece that is never captured and never has to wait for another piece to move.

What changes in a real game

  1. Captures send a piece back to base. It then needs a six to leave.
  2. A piece that waits in the column does not move. Another piece may.
  3. Pairs are safe. Two same-coloured pieces on one square cannot be taken.

Where to try it

To walk the track yourself, start a table at Boardit's Ludo. A turn lasts 30 seconds, and the host may choose Quick Ludo, with two pieces each.

Where the squares sit

The track is also regular. The four entry squares are 13 squares apart, and the starred squares sit eight squares past each entry, so every colour has the same distance to its first safe square.

The three numbers to remember

Fifty-two to go round, fifty-six to get home, five in the column.
