Party games online: eight that work on phones, from three players to twelve

Ahmed Moaz · 23 October 2026

A good party needs a game that everyone can join in thirty seconds, from whatever device they happen to have. These party games online work on phones, need no download and suit groups from three to twelve. Six of them are games I make on a free site, Boardit, so I can give exact seat limits and lengths; the other two are classics that need nothing at all. Pick one for each mood.

1. A quiz

Trivia Tavern gives every player the same multiple-choice question at the same time. Two to eight players, about fourteen minutes. The final round counts double, so nobody is out of it early. Suits any group.

2. A drawing game

Draw and Guess seats two to twelve. One player draws, everyone else types guesses, and faster guesses score more. About twenty minutes. The noisiest game on the list, and the strongest for a big group.

3. A hidden-role clue game

Who's the Spy needs three to eight players online, or up to twelve on one phone. Everyone but one player is told a secret word, and each gives a short clue. About twenty minutes. Nobody is eliminated.

4. A snakes game

Snakes and Ladders seats two to eight and takes ten to twenty-five minutes. A game of pure chance, so a child can beat a grandparent. A good opener while people arrive.

5. A dice race

Ludo seats two to four and lasts thirty to sixty minutes. More tactical than it looks: when to bring out a piece, when to hide on a safe square. Suits friends who like a rivalry.

6. A property game

Property Rush seats two to six and takes about forty-five minutes. A trading and negotiation game, with a round limit the host can set. Suits a calmer evening.

7. Twenty questions

One person thinks of something; the others ask yes-or-no questions. No equipment, any number of players.

8. Two truths and a lie

Each person shares three statements, one false. The others guess. It works on a call and takes five minutes per person.

How do you choose between them?

Count the people first. Under four: Ludo or the clue game. Four to eight: the quiz or Snakes and Ladders. Nine to twelve: the drawing game. Then think about the mood: loud, thoughtful or relaxed.

What do they have in common?

They all work on phones, they all start from a link and they all fit into an evening. A good game night online uses two or three in sequence, with a break between, and stops while people are still laughing. Online board games and party games online are the same animal when the group is friendly.

What are good house rules for party games?

Keep three. First, everyone joins before the game starts; latecomers wait for the next round. Second, mute your microphone unless you are speaking, so the call stays clear. Third, no spoilers: if you have been caught or finished, stay quiet until the end. These three rules keep a table of eight happy, and none needs enforcing if the host sets the tone with a joke.

It helps to keep a spare game in mind. If the group is flat, switch from a quiet game to a noisy one, or the reverse. A host who changes the game at the right moment is a host who saves the evening.

How do you start?

Pick a game, open its table and send the link. The first person to join is the host. Everyone else takes a seat, and the game begins. On Boardit, guests need no account, which matters for the relatives who will not install anything.

A last tip

Introduce the rules in one sentence and play a practice round. People learn party games by playing, not by listening.
