Snakes and ladders for kids at bedtime: one short game, then lights out

Ahmed Moaz · 2 November 2026

The trouble with a bedtime game is that you cannot stop it when you like. A game that ends after eight minutes is a treat, and one that is still going at the half hour is a negotiation. Snakes and ladders for kids has an advantage here. This dice game has a length that depends on the board you choose, and I can put numbers on it. I do not give sleep advice. What follows is only how long the game tends to last and how to keep it short.

Which board gives the shortest game?

Ladder Garden. The lobby offers three boards, and that choice moves the clock more than anything else. Ladder Garden carries twelve ladders against only five short snakes. Classic is the familiar layout, with ten snakes. Snake Pit has eleven and runs long.

In my calculation, with two players and the default rules, here is how many rounds each board takes. A round is one throw by each player.

The last column matters most at bedtime. On Ladder Garden, a game that drags past round 40 is a rarity. On Snake Pit it happens in nearly one game in three.

Does a six throwing again speed it up?

A little. One of the lobby switches lets a six buy another throw, with three throws as the most in a turn. On Ladder Garden with two players, my calculation puts the median at 17 rounds instead of 20, because a six now gives a bonus throw. Bounce goes the other way. When an overshoot walks to 100 and back, the median becomes 21 rounds. For a short game I would leave bounce off.

Both switches start off. For bedtime I would change only the board.

How long is that in minutes?

I cannot say, and I would not trust a site that claimed to know your child. The game page gives a range of 10 to 25 minutes across every table size, built from the number of throws a table needs and a rough pace per throw. A two-player game on Ladder Garden sits at the quick end. Each turn has a 30-second clock, so a throw never waits long, but children can take their time, and I leave that margin for you.

What keeps it to one game?

  1. Agree before the first throw that this is one game and one game only. A promised rematch is the enemy of bedtime.
  2. Seat two players. A fuller table means a longer wait between your own turns, and the game ends the moment the first counter reaches 100, with nobody playing on for second place.
  3. Leave the first-to-3 match for another night. Between two players it can take up to five games.
  4. Let the child do the throwing for their own counter, which keeps small hands busy.

A parent can set up the table on Boardit, choose Ladder Garden and invite the other player, whether that is a sibling in the next room or a grandparent playing snakes and ladders online from another house.

What if the game runs long anyway?

Then it runs long, and nobody is to blame. The game has no choices, so no player is losing because of something they did. If a child is flagging, the clock helps: when a 30-second turn runs out the table throws for them, and since a throw is a throw, the game loses nothing.

Do not announce the result of the snakes and ladders game as a verdict on the day. The winner is whoever the die favoured, and a child who loses at bedtime can be reminded that tomorrow's die is a fresh one.

One last point on timing

The numbers above are for a game that gets finished. A child who hits a long snake near the end will make a game feel longer. Say how long it will be, play one game, and put the tablet down at the end of it, not in the middle.
