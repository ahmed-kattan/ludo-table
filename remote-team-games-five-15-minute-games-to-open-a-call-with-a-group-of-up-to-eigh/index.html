Remote team games: five 15-minute games to open a call with a group of up to eight

Ahmed Moaz · 22 October 2026

Remote teams spend their days in meetings and their evenings wondering why the team does not feel like a team. A fifteen-minute game at the start of a call helps. It gets everyone talking and laughing before the agenda begins. These remote team games run in a browser, need no accounts for guests and suit groups of up to eight. They come from a free site I make, Boardit, so the seat limits and lengths are exact.

Why open with a game?

Because the first five minutes of a meeting are the most awkward. People are muting, unmuting, waiting for the host. A game fills that gap with something shared. It tells the team that the call is a place for people, not just for tasks.

Game one: a quiz

Trivia Tavern seats two to eight and takes about fourteen minutes. Everyone answers every question, so nobody sits out, and the host can choose categories that suit the team, such as Geography or Entertainment. It fits a fifteen-minute slot.

Game two: a drawing round

Draw and Guess seats up to twelve and takes about twenty minutes, so shorten it to two rounds with a quicker clock for a fifteen-minute slot. Bad drawings are a great equaliser: the intern and the director are equally hopeless.

Game three: a clue game

Who's the Spy, three to eight players, about twenty minutes. One round takes around five minutes, so play two or three rounds for a short slot. It trains listening, which is useful for teams.

Game four: a race

Snakes and Ladders, two to eight players, ten to twenty-five minutes. No decisions, so nobody can make a mistake. It is a gentle opener for a team that has had a hard week.

Game five: a property game, for a longer slot

Property Rush seats two to six and takes about forty-five minutes, with no round limit. It suits a team away day rather than a quick opener.

How do you keep it short?

Set a visible timer. Tell the team the game ends when it ends, whatever the score. Use a thirty-second turn limit, which the tables have built in, to keep the pace. Resist the urge to run over: a game that ends on time is a game the team will accept again.

How do you include everyone?

Rotate the host. Let quiet team members choose categories. Never make a game compulsory; offer an alternative role, such as scorekeeper. A team that chooses to play enjoys it more than one that is told.

What about privacy?

Use neutral topics and words. Avoid names of clients or colleagues. A game for a mixed team should be safe to screen-share.

What if the team is spread across many time zones?

Choose games that do not depend on a shared time of day. A live table needs people together, so rotate the slot to share the inconvenience. For a team of eight across three zones, a Thursday lunchtime in one place and a Thursday evening in another might be the strongest compromise. Tell the team the game will last fifteen minutes, whatever the time, and respect that promise.

Where can you run them?

Board It offers online board games and party games online for free, in a browser, with a link to join. A team can be playing within two minutes of the call starting.

What if the team is spread across time zones?

Pick the slot that is fair to the most people and rotate it. Post the link in the team channel a few minutes early, and record the scores in a shared note so anyone who missed the session can catch up. A short game at the start of a call works across zones because it asks for no preparation, only a link.

Fifteen minutes, then back to work

Start with the quiz. It is the safest, the quickest and the one that works with any team.
