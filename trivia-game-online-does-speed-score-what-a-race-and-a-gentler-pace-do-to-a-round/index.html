Trivia game online: does speed score? What a race and a gentler pace do to a round

Ahmed Moaz · 5 November 2026

Every trivia game online has to decide a small thing that changes the whole night: does answering sooner earn more? Some do not care. Others turn each question into a sprint. I had to choose for Trivia Tavern, and I ended up offering both, with the sprint as the default. The question matters most when you play online trivia with friends, because the group's mood rides on it. Here is what each choice does in points, and what the evidence says about speed in trivia questions.

What does speed pay in Trivia Tavern?

In both modes, everyone answers every question, and a right answer scores. Only the amount differs.

Take a hard question worth 300 points. In Race, the right answers are paid by their order of arrival: 300 for the first, 230 for the second, 180 for the third, and 150 for everyone after. The order is judged by when the server receives each answer, so the clock on a player's own device decides nothing. In the gentler pace, the same right answer pays 290 if it comes two seconds into a 20-second timer, and 230 if it comes at the eighteenth second. The penalty for slowness tops out at a quarter of the points.

Both are rounded to the nearest ten. An easy question is worth 100 and a medium one 200, so the gaps shrink with them.

Does a fast answer mean a right one?

Not always, and the research I found leans the other way. A study of 98 first-year engineering students across 21 Kahoot quizzes, with 4,640 recorded answers, found that wrong answers took longer to submit than right ones, in 17 of the 21 quizzes. That is classroom data, and a friendly evening is not a classroom. Still, it suggests something useful. A player who knows the answer is usually quick anyway, so a heavy speed bonus pays the same skill twice.

That is why I like the gentler pace for a mixed group. It rewards the person who knows a thing, and it does not punish the one who read the question twice.

Why do some games remove speed completely?

Kahoot added an Accuracy mode in which only correct answers count and nobody earns points for answering first. The company says the aim is to reduce pressure, and it names anxious learners among the reasons. A trivia host guide I read makes a similar point about quiz nights: speed bonuses favour teams with one fast player over teams that collaborate well, and can stress players who prefer to think.

I have not removed speed from Trivia Tavern, because a pure accuracy mode loses something real. A race has suspense that a flat score cannot give. In a trivia quiz the moment people lean in is the moment they are racing the person beside them. So the lobby keeps one switch for it.

Who should pick which mode?

Pick the race when most of your players are of a similar age, are on phones with a good connection, and like pressure. The first right answer is a small win each time, and the rest of the table can see how close they were.

Pick the gentler pace when:

  • the table spans several ages or confidence levels;
  • someone is on a weak connection or a call with a delay;
  • you want the strongest knowledge to win more often;
  • one player in the group freezes under a timer.

Whichever you choose, a player who answers right but slowly still scores in both. Nobody is shut out of a question because somebody else got there first.

Can you change your mind after one game?

Yes. The setting lives in the lobby, so one match with the race and one with the gentler pace costs nothing. Boardit's Trivia Tavern uses the same questions in both, so you can compare them fairly with the same group. I would play the first match in whichever mode you expect to like, then ask the table whether it felt rushed. A trivia night with an honest answer to that is worth more than any rule I could give you.

A rule I use when a table is split

If half the group wants speed and half does not, play the first half of the evening in the gentler pace and the second in Race. People who have already seen how the questions feel are better placed to enjoy the pressure. It also lets the quiet players score before the contest turns into a sprint.
