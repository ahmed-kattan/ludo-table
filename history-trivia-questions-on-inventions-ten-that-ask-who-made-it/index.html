History trivia questions on inventions: ten that ask who made it

Ahmed Moaz · 29 October 2026

Inventions make friendly history trivia questions, because people know the object but rarely the person. Everyone has used a telephone's descendant, yet fewer can name who built the first battery. The ten questions below form a short trivia quiz, and each asks for a name. After the answers, a short section looks at why "who invented it?" is trickier than it sounds.

The ten questions

  1. Who is credited with bringing the printing press to Europe around 1440?
  2. Which Scottish engineer improved the steam engine in the 1760s?
  3. Who built the early steam locomotive called the Rocket?
  4. Which Italian built the first electric battery in 1800?
  5. Who patented the cotton gin, a machine for separating cotton from its seeds, in 1794?
  6. Which American sold the first widely used practical sewing machines?
  7. Which French scientist worked out how to heat milk to kill germs?
  8. Which German built the three-wheeled Motorwagen in 1885?
  9. Which Italian sent the first radio signals across the Atlantic in 1901?
  10. Which Dutch scientist was the first to see bacteria, using a microscope he made himself?

The answers

  1. Johannes Gutenberg. His Bible is one of the first major books printed with movable metal type.
  2. James Watt. The unit of power, the watt, is named after him.
  3. George Stephenson, often called the "Father of Railways".
  4. Alessandro Volta. The unit of electrical voltage carries his name.
  5. Eli Whitney.
  6. Isaac Singer. His company was one of the first to sell goods on hire purchase.
  7. Louis Pasteur. The process is called pasteurisation, after him.
  8. Karl Benz. His wife, Bertha, made the first long car journey in 1888.
  9. Guglielmo Marconi.
  10. Antonie van Leeuwenhoek, who called the tiny creatures "animalcules".

Who really invented it?

Be careful with the word "invented". Watt did not invent the steam engine. The question says he improved it, and that is accurate. Singer did not build the very first sewing machine; he made one that sold widely. And Benz's Motorwagen is usually counted as the first true car, though other people were building engines and carriages at the same time.

So I word the questions as "improved", "sold" or "is credited with", and that is why the list above has no question starting "who invented the light bulb". The name people give is often a company or a better-known figure, and a fair quiz does not hide that.

Which names are the tougher ones?

Questions 2, 8 and 9 are easy, since the names are famous. Questions 3, 4 and 7 are medium. Questions 1, 5, 6 and 10 are the hard ones, because the names are known to the history-minded and not to everyone else.

A tie-break works well here, especially late in a trivia night when two people are level. Ask for the year of Volta's battery and let the closest guess take the point. The answer is 1800, a round number that is easy to say aloud.

Can you play it with friends online?

Yes. Boardit's Trivia Tavern includes a History category, one of its nine, and the host can pick it or leave the game on Mixed. Four options appear and one is correct. There are no turns, because the whole table answers together.

Every round finishes on a number question, closest guess scoring, which is the idea behind the tie-break above. For friends scattered across different countries, it is an easy way to try online trivia: someone sends round a link and each person types a name.
