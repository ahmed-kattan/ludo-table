Late to the table? How joining a Draw and Guess game that has started works

Ahmed Moaz · 9 November 2026

Some people always arrive after the game has begun. In Draw and Guess that is fine, because you can join one that is under way.

What happens when you join

You open the link, add your name and take a seat. You can guess the current drawing straight away. You will not draw during that turn, but you get your turn to draw later in the round.

Why this matters

A drawing game works best with a crowd. If everyone has to be there at the start, tables stay small. Letting people in as they come means a table can grow from three to eight in the first five minutes.

What the host should do

  1. Tell the table someone is joining. A quick hello keeps the mood.
  2. Leave the rounds open. A late player draws later in the round.
  3. Say what the word packs are. A newcomer who knows the theme can guess faster.

What a newcomer should do

Look at the drawing first. Guess a word. The first right guess scores the most, so a quick answer is worth more than a careful one.

The limits

The game takes two to twelve players. If the table is full, a newcomer cannot sit. The host can make a second table.

Where it works

Boardit's Draw and Guess allows joining a game already running, with word packs, the host's own words and a gallery of the drawings at the end. It is what many call a guess the drawing game.

Scoring as a latecomer

The first right guess keeps all of its speed points, the second about 56%, the third about 38%, so a newcomer who guesses fast can overtake a player who has been there all along. It is a good reason to join a game even when it is half over.

A word to the table

When someone joins, a short welcome costs nothing and keeps the mood. Tell them which drawing is current and what the word pack is, if you have set one. People who feel included stay for the next game.

A late guesser and the clock

A latecomer is not at risk of being dropped for being slow. The game only treats someone as away after two turns in a row end with nothing drawn, so a new arrival has two full turns before the table notices.

A small tip

If you are the late one, say so in the chat. People like to be asked to help.
