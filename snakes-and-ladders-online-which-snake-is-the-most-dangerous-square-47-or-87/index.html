Snakes and ladders online: which snake is the most dangerous? Square 47 or 87

Ahmed Moaz · 25 October 2026

Every snakes and ladders online board has ten or so snakes, and they are not equal. In the version I run there are ten. Some are on squares you hit almost every game. Others are tucked away where few counters ever land. I asked a simple question of my board: which snake does the most damage?

How did I measure a snake?

For each snake I calculated two things. First, how often a single counter lands on its head during a game. Second, how many throws the game would shorten, on average, if that snake were removed. Together they separate a snake that is merely long from one that is both long and likely to be met.

The snakes that get landed on most

On my board the snake heads you are most likely to land on, per game, are:

  1. Square 47 down to 26: 0.673 landings per game
  2. Square 49 down to 11: 0.579
  3. Square 87 down to 24: 0.525

So a counter meets the snake at 47 about two games in three. A snake at 49 is not far behind. They sit close together, which is why the stretch of board from 47 to 49 feels so dangerous: you can pass one only to find the next.

The snake that costs the most

Frequency is only half of it. The slide at 87 drops you 63 squares, and by square 87 you are near the end. Taking that snake off the board would save 7.28 throws per counter, the largest of any snake on my board. The snake at 49, with its 38-square drop, would save 4.45 throws. The snake at 47 drops only 21 squares, so despite being met most often it costs less per visit.

That gives two different answers. The most-met snake is 47. The most damaging snake is 87. If you have to pick one danger, it is the one near the end: a long fall from a place you worked hard to reach.

What does it feel like at the table?

At 87 a player has completed 87% of the journey and is a handful of throws from winning. A slide to 24 puts them near the start again and hands the lead to somebody else. It is a good reminder that this snakes and ladders game is built on swings. The earliest snakes, around 16 and 47, are an inconvenience. The latest ones are a change of fortune.

The rest of the snakes fall between these. The one at 56 drops you only three squares to 53, which hardly counts, and the one at 64 slides four squares to 60. They are the small snakes, closer to a stumble than a fall, and they show that a board can have ten snakes without ten disasters.

A fun exercise for a table

Before a game, ask each player to point to the snake they fear most and say why. After the game, count whose fear came true. You will usually find that the answers split between the frequent snake and the expensive one, which is a friendly way to talk about the difference between how often something happens and how much it matters.

Where to play and test it

If you would like to see the board with your own eyes, Boardit has a free snakes and ladders game for two to eight players, and the snakes sit exactly where the numbers above say. It works on a phone or laptop with nothing to download, so a multiplayer game with friends or family in different places takes a minute to set up. The snakes and ladders rules are the printed ones, with one throw a turn.

A note on method for two players or more

The figures treat a single counter, one throw a turn, with the exact-finish rule, and average over every possible sequence. They do not simulate other players, because in this game counters never interact: nobody can capture or block anyone else. That makes the ranking the same whether you play two players or eight, and it is why the snake ranking carries over to a table of friends.
