Games to play on FaceTime that need nothing but the call: twelve ideas

Ahmed Moaz · 27 October 2026

Sometimes you want no extra tab, no link, no page to open. You have a call, a few people and twenty spare minutes. The games to play on FaceTime in that spot are the oldest kind: you talk, you look, you guess. Here are twelve of them, grouped by what they ask of the players, with a note on the group size each suits. None of them needs anything but the call.

What counts as a game that needs nothing?

For this list, it means no download, no account, no board and no shared screen. A pen and a sheet of paper are allowed, since almost everyone has them. Each idea below can be explained in a couple of sentences, because a game that takes five minutes to explain will lose a video call.

Word and question games

  1. Twenty questions. One person thinks of an object, a person or a place. The others ask yes-or-no questions, and the answerer keeps count. It suits two to eight players.
  2. Categories. The host names a category, such as rivers or breakfast foods, and players take turns naming one. Repeat an answer or freeze, and you are out.
  3. Would you rather. Someone offers two odd choices, and everyone votes with a raised hand on camera. Perfect for a lull.
  4. A story chain. Each person adds one sentence. The story gets stranger fast, which is the pleasure of it.

Games for the camera

  1. Charades. Act out a film, an animal or a job without speaking. Use the camera well: step back so the others see your whole body.
  2. Show and tell. Everyone fetches the oddest object within reach and explains it in thirty seconds.
  3. The mystery object. Hold something very close to the lens, and the others guess what it is.
  4. A scavenger hunt. The host names a thing, such as something blue or something older than you, and the first person to show it on camera wins the point.

Games for memory and listening

  1. The packing game. The first person says "I packed a bag and in it I put..." and names an item. Each next person repeats the list and adds one.
  2. Hum the tune. One person hums a song without words, and the group guesses.

Games with paper

  1. Describe and draw. One person looks at a simple shape drawn on paper and describes it. The others draw from the description, without seeing it, and then everyone reveals their drawing. The laughter comes from how far the results stray.
  2. Hot seat. One person answers questions from everyone for two minutes, and a notepad keeps the questions fair.

How do you pick one for your group?

Match the idea to the mood. A tired group wants listening games. A lively group wants charades. Mixed ages suit the packing game. For a pair, twenty questions is hard to beat.

When would a shared table help?

There are times when you want scoring, rules and no arguments. That is when a shared web page earns its keep, the way online board games do for a regular game night. I make a free site called Boardit with six games, and guests only need a name to join. Its Draw and Guess seats up to twelve, which suits a big call far better than paper does. Who's the Spy needs at least three online seats, so it is no use for a pair.

What should a host avoid?

Do not pick a game that has one person performing while the rest stare. Do not run a round longer than ten minutes without a stretch. And do not make anyone play who prefers to watch. A shy relative on camera is still part of the evening.

Rules of thumb for the host

Start small. Try one idea, see how the group reacts, and then move on. Keep a list of three spare games in your head. Games to play on FaceTime need no equipment, but they do need a person willing to say "right, here is how it works" and then stop talking.
