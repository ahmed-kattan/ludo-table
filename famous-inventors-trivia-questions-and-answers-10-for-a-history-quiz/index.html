Famous inventors trivia questions and answers: 10 for a history quiz

Ahmed Moaz · 18 October 2026

Behind every gadget there is a person, and the stories of famous inventors are full of accidents, rivalries and stubbornness. They also make good quiz material. Here are ten famous inventors trivia questions and answers, from the easy to the tricky. Use them on paper, or as a history round in a trivia quiz online with friends.

The questions

  1. Who invented the World Wide Web?
  2. Which car maker's Model T made cars affordable for ordinary people?
  3. Which life-saving medicine did Alexander Fleming discover in 1928?
  4. Which Scottish inventor demonstrated the first working television?
  5. Which woman is considered the first computer programmer?
  6. Who created the first vaccine, against smallpox, in 1796?
  7. What did Wilhelm Röntgen discover in 1895, letting doctors see bones?
  8. Which British engineer built the Great Western Railway and huge steamships?
  9. Which ancient Greek worked out how to measure volume while taking a bath?
  10. Whose Difference Engine was an early mechanical computer design?

The answers, with a note on each

  1. Tim Berners-Lee. He never patented the web, so anyone could use it for free.
  2. Ford. Henry Ford's assembly line cut the time to build a car from 12 hours to about 90 minutes.
  3. Penicillin, often called the world's first true antibiotic.
  4. John Logie Baird. His first television image was a ventriloquist's dummy called Stooky Bill.
  5. Ada Lovelace, the daughter of the poet Lord Byron.
  6. Edward Jenner. The word vaccine comes from vacca, Latin for cow, because he used cowpox.
  7. X-rays. His first X-ray image showed his wife's hand and wedding ring.
  8. Isambard Kingdom Brunel. His ship, the SS Great Eastern, was the biggest in the world for 40 years.
  9. Archimedes. Legend says he ran through the streets shouting Eureka.
  10. Charles Babbage. A working Difference Engine was finally built from his plans in 1991.

How hard is it?

Questions 1 to 3 are familiar. Questions 4 to 7 are medium and good for teams. Questions 8 to 10 are hard. If your group is young, swap Brunel and Babbage for the questions about the Wright brothers and the telephone, which are easy.

How do you fit it into a trivia night?

For a family evening, play it as a single round and let the youngest read out the answers. Children enjoy the drama of the reveal more than the scoring.

Where can you play inventors trivia online?

It is online trivia with friends, and it needs no download.

What makes a good inventor question?

A name people half-know, a date that is just out of reach and a small human detail. The strongest of them make the table say "I didn't know that" and then repeat it at dinner.

What do inventors have in common?

Curiosity, mostly, an appetite for tinkering, and a willingness to be wrong in public. Fleming's mould, Spencer's chocolate and Jenner's cowpox all began with something unexpected that somebody noticed. A quizmaster can ask the table which of the ten they would have liked to meet, and why. The answers say more about the table than the quiz does.

Follow-up questions

Ask which inventor held over a thousand patents, who made the first long-distance car journey and which country invented the compass. They are in the same bank, and they make good bonus questions when a team is ahead.
