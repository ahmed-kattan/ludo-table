Snake and ladder game online free: how to start a table with friends in a browser

Ahmed Moaz · 19 October 2026

In much of India and across South Asia, the game is simply called snake and ladder, singular, and it is the first board game most children learn. If you want to play it with relatives who live in another city, you do not need to post a board. You can set up a free snake and ladder game online in a couple of minutes. Here is what to look for, so that the game you get is the one you remember.

What does a free online snake and ladder game need?

A good snakes and ladders game online does a few plain things well:

  1. A shared board. Everyone should see the same squares and the same counters at the same moment.
  2. A real die. The throw should be random and visible to all, not chosen by a player.
  3. The rules you expect. Ladders climb, snakes slide, and the last square has to be reached with an exact number or by a rule you all agree on.
  4. A turn limit. Someone will answer the door. A clock stops the whole table from waiting.
  5. No sign-up wall. Grandparents are put off by forms. A shared link is easier.

Which rules are worth asking about?

Households play the game in several slightly different ways, so it pays to ask before you start. In the version I built:

  • One throw per turn. A six does not give you another go.
  • You must land on 100 exactly. If you throw too many, your counter stays where it is.
  • Each turn is capped at thirty seconds. If you run out, the table throws for you.
  • Two to eight people can sit at one table.

If your family plays with an extra throw on a six, or with bouncing back from the end, say so when you invite people. Arguments about rules are the main way a friendly game goes wrong.

How long will a game last?

Plan on about half an hour with three or four people, and longer with more. A single counter takes about 39 throws on average, and a table of four finishes after roughly 19 rounds. The median game is shorter than the mean because a few unlucky snakes stretch the long tail.

How do you invite people?

You open the game page, make a table, and send the link or the short code to the others. They open it on a phone or a laptop and take a seat. That is all it takes to play snakes and ladders online with friends, as a two-player match or a multiplayer game of up to eight. On Boardit this takes a minute and nobody needs an account, and the snakes and ladders rules are the printed ones. If you are mixing generations, it helps to do a practice move together by phone first, so that everyone knows where the die button is.

What if somebody has a poor connection?

A timer protects you here too. If a player drops out for a moment, the turn is thrown for them and the game goes on. When they return, they pick up where the counter is. For a family call, ask people to keep the page open and to mute the video if the connection is slow, since the game itself needs very little data.

Can children play it alone?

Yes, but the table is more fun when someone reads out each move. Ask the child to say "I am on 37, I threw a 4, I go to 41". Counting aloud is good practice, and it keeps the others in the loop when a screen is small.

Family etiquette that helps

  • Agree on the rule for the last square before you start.
  • Let the youngest player throw first in the opening game.
  • Say thank you for every ladder, even someone else's.
  • Keep the chat friendly; the die has no feelings to hurt.

What this does not replace

A printed board has its own pleasures: the rattle of the die, the counters you can hold. An online table is a way to share the game across distance, not a replacement for a rug and a cup of tea. Use it for the evenings when you cannot be in the same room, and keep the box for the ones when you can.
