TV trivia questions about sitcoms: ten with answers, from Seinfeld to Ted Lasso

Ahmed Moaz · 3 November 2026

Most people can name a sitcom they have seen ten times. Fewer can say how it was made. These TV trivia questions go behind the screen: who rescued a cancelled show, who checked the science, and where a famous sofa came from. There are ten, with a one-line answer and a reference in the front matter for each, and none asks you to remember a plot. That makes the set a friendly trivia quiz for people who watch a lot of TV and for people who do not.

Ten questions behind the screen

  1. Which sitcom about "nothing" was co-created by Jerry Seinfeld and Larry David?
  2. In which season of How I Met Your Mother did we learn the mother's name, Tracy?
  3. Which network picked up Brooklyn Nine-Nine a day after Fox cancelled it in 2018?
  4. Which actor became a star in Parks and Recreation before moving on to big films?
  5. Who checked the equations written on the whiteboards in The Big Bang Theory?
  6. The American Office is a remake of whose British series?
  7. Where was the orange sofa in Central Perk found?
  8. How many monkeys played Marcel in Friends?
  9. In The Fresh Prince of Bel-Air, what first name does the main character use?
  10. Ted Lasso began as a character in short promotional spots for coverage of which league?

The answers

  1. Seinfeld. It was co-created by its star and Larry David.
  2. The final season. Until then she was known only as the mother.
  3. NBC.
  4. Chris Pratt, who went on to star in Guardians of the Galaxy.
  5. A real physicist.
  6. Ricky Gervais's.
  7. In the basement of the Warner Bros. studio.
  8. Two. Capuchin monkeys named Katie and Monkey shared the role.
  9. Will. The character uses the actor's real first name.
  10. The Premier League, shown in the United States on NBC.

Which sitcom question gets the loudest answer?

Number 7. Many players will say "Central Perk", the place, and then realise the question asks about the sofa. A host who reads it slowly gives the table a fair chance, and the basement answer is a good surprise.

Number 3 comes second. The rescue of Brooklyn Nine-Nine happened a day after the cancellation, which shows how quickly a TV decision can turn around. It makes a short story you can tell in a breath.

Question 5 is the one I would give to a younger player. Nobody has to know anything about physics to guess that a science sitcom would ask a scientist. A fair quiz has at least one question where a smart guess beats knowledge.

How do you pace a round of sitcom questions?

Start with 1, 6 and 9, which most players can reason out. Move to 4, 7 and 10, where the name is easy but the detail is not. Leave 2, 3, 5 and 8 for later, since each hangs on one fact that you either know or do not.

If you are using this set at a family trivia night, mix it with a round of music or geography, so the player who never watches TV has a way back into the game.

Can you play these as trivia online with friends?

Boardit's Trivia Tavern has an Entertainment category among its nine, which suits a night of TV and film questions. Players join from a browser and answer at the same moment, each on a personal device. For people in different cities, that makes a TV round feel like being on the same sofa, with none of the shouting over each other that a real living room brings.
