What to say between questions: a host's script for Trivia Tavern

Ahmed Moaz · 17 November 2026

A quiz is half the questions and half the host. A good host fills the pauses with short remarks. This is the script I would use. It takes no preparation.

Before the first question

"Four answers, one right. No help and no skipping. The first right answer scores the most. Every round ends with a number, and the closest wins. The last round counts double."

Between rounds one and two

"Scores are on the board. Round two gets harder. Remember, the last round is worth double, so nobody is out."

Before the number question

"This one is a number. A year, a count. You do not need to be sure. The nearest guess wins a bonus."

After the number question

"Who was closest? Exact earns everything, close earns most, and being near still scores."

Before the last round

"Everything is doubled. A lead of a few hundred points can disappear. Take your time with the hard ones."

If the wager final is on

"One more question. Its category is on the screen. You can stake nothing, a quarter, half or all of your score. You have 20 seconds."

At the end

"Thank you, everyone. The winner has the bragging rights until the next night."

Tips for delivery

  1. Keep it short. The questions are the show.
  2. Do not explain scoring in the middle. Once at the start is enough.
  3. Praise a near miss. It encourages the next guess.
  4. Be fair about a tie. Sudden death settles it.

Length

A default game is 3 rounds of 10 questions at 20 seconds, and the table log (13 September to 30 October 2026) gives 10.0 minutes as the median for a finished one.

Where to host

To run the script with real players, open a table on Boardit's Trivia Tavern; it takes two to eight.

For a shy host

If you do not enjoy talking, read the lines straight from the screen. The game explains most of itself. A host who says very little and keeps the pace up is still a good host, and the table will remember the quiz, not the commentary.

Reading out a stake

If the wager final is on, read the options aloud. A player on 3,000 can stake nothing, 750, 1,500 or all 3,000, and a script that says the numbers helps a player who does not want to do the sum.

The script in a line

Short, kind and clear about the last round.
