Eight safe squares out of 52: the geometry of danger in Ludo

Ahmed Moaz · 9 November 2026

Ludo is a game of captures, so the question is where a piece is safe. The track has 52 squares. Eight of them are safe: the four squares where pieces enter, and four stars. That is 15.4% of the track.

How a capture happens

A piece can capture another piece by landing on its square, unless the square is safe. A piece sitting one to six squares ahead of an opponent's piece can be hit by exactly one throw, and the chance of that is one in six, or 16.7%.

Further away

A piece seven to twelve squares ahead cannot be reached in one throw. The opponent needs a six first, which brings another throw, and then the right number. The chance of that is one in 36, or 2.8%, and it ignores the rule that three sixes in a row lose the turn.

So there is a cliff in the odds. A piece six squares ahead is in danger. A piece seven squares ahead is nearly safe. One square of distance changes the risk from 17% to under 3%.

What this means for play

  1. Run past six squares. If you must stay in range, make it a safe square.
  2. Check behind you, not in front. A piece is hit from behind. What matters is who stands within six squares behind you.
  3. Use the stars and entry squares as stops. Resting on one costs a move and removes the risk.
  4. Use the same cliff against others. Sitting seven squares behind a piece does little. Sitting five does a lot.

What I have not counted

These are one-throw odds for one pair of pieces. In a real game several opponents may be near, and each one adds to the danger. I have not computed that.

Where the stars are

The safe squares are not scattered. The four entry squares are 13 squares apart on the 52-square track, and each starred square sits eight squares past an entry, so a piece that has just left base is within a short walk of the next safe square.

The takeaway

Six is the number to remember. Within six squares, a piece is in danger. Beyond twelve, it is almost always safe.

The track and the safe squares come from the game's rules, read on 17 October 2026.
