A trivia night to raise money for a club: a plan that fits an hour

Ahmed Moaz · 15 November 2026

A club or a team often wants a social evening, and a quiz is the easiest to organise. This is a plan for an hour of Trivia Tavern, with everything done by link.

Before

  1. Pick a host. One person opens the table, picks the settings and reads the rules.
  2. Choose the format. Trivia Tavern seats two to eight. For a bigger club, run several tables and compare the winners.
  3. Send the link. A guest needs only a name.
  4. Decide what the prize is. Anything from bragging rights to a small gift.

The format

Three games of about ten minutes each. In Boardit's table log, the median finished game took 10.0 minutes, for games from 13 September to 30 October 2026. With introductions and breaks, the evening fits an hour.

TimeWhat
0 to 10Welcome, joining, rules
10 to 20Game one, Mixed
20 to 25Break
25 to 35Game two, chosen categories
35 to 40Break
40 to 50Game three, with the final bet
50 to 60Results, prizes, next date

Keeping it fair

Use Mixed in the first game, so nobody is at a disadvantage. In the second, let the table vote on categories. In the third, switch on the final bet so the leader cannot rest.

Extra rules

  1. A sudden-death tiebreak. It is on by default.
  2. Teams. Let two members share a phone, so one phone is one team.
  3. No lookups. The clock is 20 seconds, which makes cheating hard.

The scores

Everyone can see the scores all the time. Read the top three at the end of each game.

Where to play

Boardit has Trivia Tavern free in a browser.

After the evening

Send the final scores to the group in a message, with the date of the next night. A club that sees a result and a date comes back. One that hears nothing assumes it was a one-off.

The categories for a club

A club has its own interests, so pick the categories for the room. General knowledge has 387 questions and Sports 336, Science 315 and Geography 311, so a night on those four has plenty to draw on.

The evening in a line

Three games, two breaks, one winner.
