What happens when you cannot pay rent in Property Rush

Ahmed Moaz · 7 November 2026

In many trading games, missing a payment ends your game on the spot. In Property Rush it does not. Since 2 October 2026, if you cannot pay the rent you owe, you owe a debt instead, and you get to deal with it.

What a debt is

When a player lands on your city and cannot pay the rent, the unpaid amount becomes a debt. The player is not out. They must clear the debt by raising money. There are three ways.

  1. Mortgage. Borrow against a property you own. A mortgage pays half the price, and the property stops collecting rent until you lift it.
  2. Sell. Sell buildings or a property back for cash.
  3. Trade. Offer a property to another player in exchange for money.

Lifting a mortgage costs the mortgage plus about 10%. A city with a mortgage of 200, for instance, is lifted at 221, because the game rounds up.

Bankruptcy

If you cannot raise the money, you can declare bankruptcy. It is final: you leave the game. You can also walk away. The last player who still has money wins, and there is no round limit.

What it changes in play

A debt gives a player time to recover. It also gives the player who is owed money a decision. You can trade with a debtor, ease their position, or let them sink.

A worked example

Suppose you owe 150 and have 80 in cash. You are 70 short. Giza has a price of 100, so mortgaging it pays 50. Alexandria has a price of 110, so it pays 55. Together that is 105, which clears the debt with 35 to spare. Lifting the Giza mortgage later costs about 10% more than it paid, which the game rounds to 56.

You lose the rent from those two cities until you lift them, and that is the real price of a debt.

Why this suits a long game

A trading game can take most of an evening. Instant elimination turns a long game into a long wait for the first player out. A debt keeps that player in the game, makes them negotiate, and sometimes produces a turn the table remembers.

What a debt costs against the bank

The size of a debt follows the board. Airports pay 25, 50, 100 or 200 depending on how many of the four the owner holds, and a hotel in Toronto charges 2,000. The first is a small bill to run up; the second is more than most purses. While you owe, you cannot roll, so the real cost of a large debt is that your turn is paused until you raise the money.

Where to play it

I think the debt rule is kinder and more interesting than instant elimination, and it gives a losing player something to do. Game nights go better when no one has to sit out an hour.
