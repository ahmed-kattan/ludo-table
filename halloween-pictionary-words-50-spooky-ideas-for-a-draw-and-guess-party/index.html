Halloween pictionary words: 50 spooky ideas for a draw and guess party

Ahmed Moaz · 18 October 2026

Halloween parties need an activity that works for children in costumes, parents with a drink and the odd teenager who says they are too old. A drawing game does the job. These Halloween pictionary words are chosen to be easy to sketch and a little spooky without being frightening. Use them on paper, or paste them into a draw and guess table online with friends who cannot come to the party.

What makes a good Halloween word?

A clear outline, a hint of fun and no real fear. A pumpkin, a bat, a witch's hat: all shapes a child can draw in seconds. Add a few actions for the older players, and keep the scary level to a giggle.

How do you run a spooky round?

Dim the lights, put a lantern on the table and give every player a slip. One minute per drawing is enough. Use the creatures for the first round, the things for the second and the actions for the last, when the group has loosened up.

Give a small prize for the scariest drawing, and another for the silliest. Children love both.

How do you keep it friendly?

Avoid words that frighten the youngest. A ghost with a smile is funnier than a ghost with teeth. If a child hesitates, offer a simpler word. Make a rule that the drawings can be scary but the voices cannot.

Can you play it online?

Yes. At Boardit, a free site I make, you can add this list as custom words and play a drawing game online with friends who are not at the party. Up to twelve can join in a browser, and the table works well on a video call.

What if you want a longer game?

Mix in easy words for the youngest and harder ones for the adults. A mixed list of 100 words keeps a large party going for an hour.

For a party with a costume contest, hold the drawing game while the judges decide. It gives the room something to do, and the judges can enjoy the scoring without pressure. Take a photo of each winning drawing next to its artist, because the pictures will make better souvenirs than any trophy.

If you have a tablet to hand, let the youngest children draw their word on it with a finger. They will be proud to show the screen to the whole table, and a parent can help guess in the chat.

Keep it silly

A final note on tone: if a guest dislikes a word, they can swap it. Nobody should be made to draw something that frightens them on a night meant for fun.

Halloween games work when they are more silly than scary. A skeleton with a wobbly arm will get a bigger laugh than a masterpiece.
