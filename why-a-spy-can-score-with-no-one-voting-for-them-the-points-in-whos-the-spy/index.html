Why a spy can score with no one voting for them: the points in Who's the Spy

Ahmed Moaz · 13 November 2026

The points in Who's the Spy are simple, and they give the spy a real way to win. This is how they work, with the arithmetic.

The three ways to score

  1. Vote for a spy: 1 point.
  2. Be a spy that nobody votes for: 1 point.
  3. Be a lone spy who names the word: one extra point.

Most points after the last round takes the game.

What that does to a spy

A spy who is never accused scores a point without doing anything else. A caught spy can still score by naming the word, provided they are alone. So a spy has two routes to a point: staying hidden, or guessing.

The civilians' side

Each civilian earns a point for naming a spy. A civilian who is wrong earns nothing, but loses nothing. There is no penalty for a bad vote.

Expected points

If every voter picked another player at random, a voter at a table of six would name a lone spy one time in five, so would expect 0.2 points a round from that rule. A spy would escape unnamed if nobody picked them, which is likely for a spy who blends in.

What it suggests

  1. For civilians: a vote costs nothing, so cast one every time.
  2. For the spy: the aim is to receive no votes. A vote on someone else is fine.
  3. For a lone spy who is caught: the last guess is worth a point. Listen to the clues you heard, and choose the word that fits all of them.

The final guess

A caught lone spy picks from ten words: the real one and nine decoys of the same category. A blind pick is right one time in ten.

A random baseline

If every voter at a table of eight voted at random, each would expect 0.43 points from naming a spy over three rounds. A spy's own points come from not being caught, so the baseline is what a spy is up against.

A balance

The spy usually has a chance. A table that votes together catches them often, and a spy that mixes in escapes often. That is the game.
