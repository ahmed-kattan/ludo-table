A Diwali Ludo night with family in different cities, without another app

Ahmed Moaz · 10 October 2026

Diwali falls on Sunday 8 November 2026, according to the Government of India's holiday list. For a family split between, say, Delhi, Leicester and New Jersey, that leaves one hard question: how do you play together? A Diwali Ludo night suits a family that already knows the game, from the oldest aunt to the youngest cousin. It needs no new app, as long as it runs in a browser. What it does need is planning for clocks and seats.

When is Diwali 2026, and what time is it for everyone?

Sunday 8 November is the date on that central list, which also notes that some states mark Diwali a day earlier. The clocks are the catch. The UK clocks go back on 25 October and the US clocks on 1 November, while India has no daylight saving at all. Indian Standard Time is UTC+5:30, a half-hour offset that is easy to get wrong.

So the time difference between India and UK is four and a half hours until 25 October and five and a half on Diwali itself. The time difference between India and USA, on the east coast, grows from nine and a half to ten and a half hours on 1 November. If anyone asks when do the clocks go back, those two dates are the answer.

The later slot is kinder to California and harder on Indian grandparents. There is no slot that suits everyone, so choose who gets the early morning before anyone books the call.

How many players can play Ludo? Four to a board

A Ludo board has four corners, and the version I build enforces it. Boardit's Ludo takes between two and four players, and it refuses to start with a fifth player seated. So, can 8 people play Ludo? Yes, on two boards of four, side by side.

The arithmetic for bigger families:

  • 8 people: two boards of four.
  • 9 people: three boards of three, rather than 4 + 4 with somebody left out.
  • 10 people: 4 + 3 + 3.

Mix the generations on each board rather than seating the children together. Ludo with family is more fun when a grandparent sends a grandchild home and the grandchild returns the favour, and the same seating works for Ludo with friends.

Seating a Diwali Ludo night across cities

Open the boards when the call starts, not an hour before. A room left untouched in the lobby stays open for ten minutes, which is long enough for a cousin who is still finding the link, and short enough that a room opened at lunchtime will be gone by the evening.

Send the room code in the family chat that only family can read, never on a public post. Each relative joins from their own device.

Ludo without an app matters for relatives who have one phone, little storage and no wish to sign up for anything. To play Ludo online with family, keep the Diwali video call running in its own app beside the game, so the board and the faces share the evening.

When somebody has to leave early

Diwali evenings have plans of their own. UNESCO's description of the festival includes lighting lamps, offering prayers and sharing meals. Somebody will need to go before the board is finished.

Since 3 October 2026, a player whose four pieces are all home can leave the table while the others play on for the remaining places. That suits a grandparent who finishes first and wants to get back to the evening at home, or a cousin in America whose morning has other plans. Agree beforehand who will stay to the end of each board.

A simple running order

  1. Greetings on the call, with a moment for anyone who wants to show their lamps or sweets.
  2. Split into boards, mixing ages, and play one game each.
  3. Winners of each board play one final game together.
  4. Close the call before the evening's own plans begin.

Among Diwali games, Ludo on a video call has one advantage in a family that already plays it: nobody spends the call explaining the Ludo rules.

Before you send the invitation

I have not timed a Diwali evening like this, so the plan gives no game length. The time table is arithmetic for the cities named; check it against where your own family lives.
