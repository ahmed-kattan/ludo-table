Mixed or chosen? How the category setting changes a Trivia night

Ahmed Moaz · 13 November 2026

The first choice a host makes in Trivia Tavern is the categories. There are nine, and a Mixed setting that brings in all of them. The choice sounds small, and it sets the mood of an evening.

What Mixed does

On Mixed, every category comes up equally. Over a game of 30 questions, a table sees roughly three or four from each of the nine. Nobody has to know a topic well. They only need to know a little of everything.

What chosen categories do

The host picks the subjects. A table that picks music and film gets a night about music and film. The questions in each category are still about well-known things and are not too hard, so a chosen set does not turn into an exam.

Which suits which table

  1. Mixed company: Mixed. Nobody is left out.
  2. A group with a shared interest: chosen categories. A table of football fans can pick sports.
  3. A family: Mixed or a vote, and avoid anything one person always wins.
  4. A team: one game on Mixed, one on a chosen set.

The trap

A chosen category that suits one person makes the others watch. If you are the host and you are the expert, skip your own subject.

Voting on categories

I like to let the table vote for two or three before the first game. It spreads the effort. Players who chose a category are more engaged when it comes up.

Difficulty is separate

The difficulty setting is separate from the categories. By default it shuffles easy, medium and hard, so a round starts easy and ends harder. Easy questions are worth 100 points, medium 200 and hard 300.

What stays the same

Whatever you choose, every round ends with a number question, and the last round counts double.

What chosen categories narrow

Narrowing has a cost in repeats. Music holds 275 questions, History 248 and Video games 187, and a night on one of them draws from a pool a fraction of the size of a Mixed one, so questions come round sooner if the same group plays again.

A rule I use

One game on Mixed to warm up, one on chosen categories to settle in.
