A drawing game for two: a long-distance couple's version

Ahmed Moaz · 17 October 2026

Most party games fall flat with two people, because the fun comes from the crowd, and most games for long distance couples are quizzes or card games for that reason. A drawing game for two is different, and the reason is in the scoring. I build a small game site, and its drawing game pays the person drawing a share of every guess. With only two of you, that turns a contest into something closer to a team sport played across a video call.

Who it suits

It suits a couple who already call each other most evenings and want the call to be about something for twenty minutes. For anyone short of long distance date night ideas, it is an easy one: no drawing talent, no shared room and nothing to install.

The game starts with as few as two players: one draws, one guesses, then you swap. That is all draw and guess for 2 players needs, plus a device each. Because it starts at two, you can play it with friends another night too.

Why the scoring makes you teammates

I would call Boardit's drawing game half co-operative. Whoever is drawing collects part of the points whenever the guess comes quickly, so on every turn you both want the same thing: a fast right answer.

There is also no race between guessers. A first correct guess keeps its full speed points, and with two players every correct guess is the first. Speed is the only thing that moves your score.

Here are the numbers for an 80-second drawing. A right guess with 60 seconds still on the clock earns 400 points. At the halfway mark, it earns 300.

The odd result in a drawing game for two: the clearer artist loses

Here is the part couples argue about. Suppose you each draw three times. Your partner draws clearly, and you guess every one of their drawings with 60 seconds left: 400 points each, 1,200 in all. You draw like a tired child, and they only get yours with half the clock left: 300 each, 900 in all.

You both also earn a share of what the other scored on your drawings. But you finish ahead, because you guessed faster, and you guessed faster because their drawings were easier to read.

Put briefly, I think the scores only separate on whose drawings are easier to read and who types faster. So in a two player drawing game, being the better artist helps your partner win.

Play for a joint score instead

That paradox points to a better format for long distance relationship games, and for drawing games for couples in general. Ignore who won. Add your two totals together and treat that as the couple's score for the night.

Every fast guess raises it, whoever is guessing. Every clear drawing raises it, whoever is drawing. Write the total down and try to beat it next time you play. It is the rare online drawing game where both of you are pulling the same way for the whole evening, which is more than most online games for couples manage.

Two practical touches help. First, take turns hosting, so the same person is not always the one setting up the room. Second, keep the call open for the laughing. A drawing game on a video call is mostly about the faces people pull at a bad sketch.

If the call cuts out

Long-distance calls drop. The game keeps your guesses when the connection fails and sends them again when it returns. The clock does not wait, though, so a long drop still costs time and points on that drawing.

The drawing game itself is free on Board It, a small site of free games I run.

Before you rely on any of this

The worked numbers are my own arithmetic from the game's scoring rules, and the figures are hypothetical. I have no record of couples playing, and no figures on how close two-player games usually are. Treat the joint score as a suggestion for a friendly couples game night, not a finding, and as one of many games to play with your partner online.
