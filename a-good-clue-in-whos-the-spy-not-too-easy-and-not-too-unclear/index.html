A good clue in Who's the Spy: not too easy and not too unclear

Ahmed Moaz · 10 November 2026

In Who's the Spy the clue is the whole skill. You describe the secret word without saying it. A clue that is too easy lets the spy work out the word. One that is too vague makes you look like the spy.

The two failures

Too easy. You say something that only fits one word. The spy hears it, and now they know. You have helped the other side.

Too unclear. You say something so vague that it could fit anything. The table looks at you and asks why. You have made yourself a suspect.

A good clue sits between them. It is true of the word, and also of several other words.

The limits

Online you get 25 seconds and 20 characters for a clue. That is short on purpose. There is no space to explain.

Five habits

  1. Say something concrete, not a definition. A property, a place or a use.
  2. Avoid the obvious first thought. The spy has thought of it too.
  3. Listen to the clues before yours. If someone has said something you would have said, say something different.
  4. Be consistent. A clue that does not match your first one looks like a bluff.
  5. If you are the spy, borrow. Echo the shape of an earlier clue and add a plausible detail.

What the spy knows

The spy knows only the category. They have to work out the word from the clues, and give a clue that fits.

The vote

A vote can be called after two laps of clues. You get a point for voting for a spy. A spy that nobody votes for scores a point. A lone spy who names the word earns one more.

Where to practise

Practise clues at a table on Boardit's Who's the Spy, online or on one phone. If you know the game as the imposter game, the clue rules above are the same.

The clock for a whole round

The limits add up to a budget. With two laps of clues before a vote can be called, three players need at most two and a half minutes, and each extra player adds 50 seconds. A clue that fits in 20 characters and 25 seconds keeps that budget honest.

Between too easy and too unclear

Aim for a clue that makes your friends nod and the spy hesitate.
